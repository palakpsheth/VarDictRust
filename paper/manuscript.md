## Abstract

### Background
`VarDict` (`VarDictJava` v1.8.3) is a heuristic-aware local realigner and somatic/germline variant caller widely used in clinical oncology and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) minimal residual disease (MRD) pipelines. Streaming alignments through a Java pileup engine piped to external R and Perl scripts causes unbounded per-interval memory growth and runtime crashes on deep sequencing, while `vardictcpp` omits splicing, amplicon VCF, structural variant, and CRAM modes.

### Results
We present `VarDictRust` (`v1.1.0`; `60`-experiment cloud study at `v1.0.6`), a single-binary, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` Rust consolidation built under [rewrites.bio](https://rewrites.bio). Replicating R's 5-decimal `HALF_EVEN` Fisher exact test, default `--compat=vardictjava` matches `VarDictJava v1.8.3 (0886261)` (`ε = 1e-6`) across `13/13` operational modes (`95%` Clopper-Pearson confidence interval [CI] `75.29%–100.00%`), `17/17` modalities (`95%` CI `80.49%–100.00%`), `18/18` slices (`95%` CI `81.47%–100.00%`), `18/18` `-f 0` noise-floor sweeps (`95%` CI `81.47%–100.00%`), and all completing paired runs across `10` short-read chemistries (`70/70` GA4GH `v3.5` truth strata). On contiguous chromosome 1 (`249.25` Mbp), bounded `O(tile + halo)` streaming finishes in `9.75` s and `318.6` MB resident memory (`51.5`× faster and `342.2`× leaner than `VarDictJava` `-Xmx200g`; `10.9`× faster and `34.0`× leaner than `vardictcpp ae93fe39dc (Wolff 2026)`). Within `128` GiB (`n2-standard-32`; `-Xmx200g` baselines on `256` GiB `n2-highmem-32`), `VarDictRust` completes unchunked `130.8` GB `HG002` (`5.05` GB RAM) and `329.0` GB `364`× `HG008` somatic WGS (`12.43` GB RAM), where `VarDictJava` runs out of memory and `vardictcpp` times out, gaining `+5.01%` SNV and `+17.26%` indel recall on `NIST GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100` (`+17.47%` and `+27.98%` on `CMRG v1.00`). Opt-in `--compat=off` (`32` switches) and `--preset` (`wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, `ctdna`) resolve all `11,612` targeted VCF schema/canonicalization findings across `19` callsets (`19/19` `bcftools norm` pass) and rescue `+13/41` (`+31.7` pp) `GT=0/0`-masked `SEQC2`-slice somatic calls in ploidy-strict `vcfeval` (`+0.1538` optimal $F_1$).

### Conclusions
`VarDictRust` consolidates a three-language variant-calling pipeline (`102` Conda packages) into a `4.56` MiB static Linux binary with default oracle equivalence (`ε = 1e-6`), bounded streaming memory, and opt-in VCFv4.2/v4.3 clinical presets across targeted panels, RNA-seq splicing, unchunked deep WGS, and `0.5%` VAF assays.

**Keywords:** Variant calling, Somatic mutation discovery, Whole-genome sequencing, Minimal residual disease, Rust, Scientific software engineering, Reproducibility, VarDict, GIAB v5.0q, HG008

## Background

Clinical oncology and liquid-biopsy assays must detect somatic SNVs, multinucleotide variants (MNVs), indels, and structural variants (SVs) across variable tumor purities and cell-free DNA (cfDNA) fractions down to `0.5%`–`1.0%` VAF [1, 2], whereas germline variant callers such as `FreeBayes` [3], `Strelka2` [4], `Octopus` [5], and `DeepVariant` [6] model diploid genotype likelihoods [7, 8]. Introduced by Lai et al. in 2016 [1], `VarDict` (`VarDictJava` v1.8.3) integrates local soft-clip and indel realignment (`CigarModifier` and `VariationRealigner`). It adds composite MNP phasing, amplicon primer-edge filtering (`-a`), RNA-seq splice-junction accounting (`-i`), and inline SV breakpoint assembly. `VarDict` is deployed across clinical panel pipelines, `bcbio-nextgen`, `nf-core/sarek`, and multi-caller somatic consensus ensembles [9, 10].

Reference `VarDictJava` v1.8.3 has four structural limits on deep WGS and cloud infrastructure:

1. **Three-Language Pipeline and IPC Overhead.** `VarDictJava` emits an intermediate `36`- to `61`-column tab-delimited `.var` stream. Column counts are `Simple` `36/38`, `Amplicon` `38/40`, and `Somatic` `55/61` without/with `--fisher`. That stream pipes to an R script (`teststrandbias.R` or `testsomatic.R`) for Fisher exact testing. It then pipes to a Perl script (`var2vcf_valid.pl` or `var2vcf_paired.pl`) for filtering and VCF 4.2 formatting (`Figure 1`). Even when `--fisher` runs inside Java, the Perl post-processor remains mandatory, requiring `102` Conda packages across OpenJDK, R (`statmod`), and Perl.

2. **Unbounded JVM Heap Retention on Contiguous Spans.** `VarDictJava` allocates per-position pileup maps (`Map<Integer, VariationMap<String, Variation>>` and reference base arrays) across the full BED interval or `-R` region. Variant finalization (`ToVarsBuilder`) and realignment (`VariationRealigner`) only run afterwards. On megabase or chromosome-scale intervals, RSS grows linearly with interval length (`O(L × depth)`). That causes `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError` crashes or garbage-collection stalls above `100`–`200` GiB RAM (`Table 2`, `Table 5`).

3. **Runtime Exceptions and Non-Determinism on Edge-Case Reads.** Over `30` upstream `VarDictJava` issues report `CigarModifier.modifyCigar` crashes. Affected issues include `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#7`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#38`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#187`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#197`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#243`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#249`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#253`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#282`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#283`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#320`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#341`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#380`, and `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#384`. The crashes hit supplementary or heavily soft-clipped reads with `NullPointerException`, `StringIndexOutOfBoundsException`, and `ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException`. Other issues cover non-deterministic row ordering from unseeded `java.util.HashMap` iteration (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#282`), broken CRAM decoding (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#249`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#380`), and truncated `##contig` VCF headers (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#320`).

4. **Incomplete C++ Port (`vardictcpp`).** Wolff (2026) introduced `vardictcpp ae93fe39dc (Wolff 2026)`, a C++/htslib reimplementation targeting single-sample and paired somatic speedups [11]. Our 3-way audit identifies 12 functional and statistical divergences in `vardictcpp` (`Table 8`; `Supplementary Table S6`). Specifically, `vardictcpp` omits `##contig` and `INFO/END` VCF headers. It omits CRISPR (`-J`/`-j`), structural variant (`--nosv` forced by default), and RNA splice (`-i`) modes. It emits raw tab-separated text instead of VCF in amplicon (`-a`) mode. It rejects concatenated CLI flags. It diverges on CRAM decoding (`-G`), `b37` numeric contigs, and MNP phasing/tie-breaking. It consumes unbounded memory on large intervals (`32.68` GB on HG002 WGS; `94.06` GB with a `3`-hour timeout on HG008 WGS). Pre-chunked regions gain `+309,569` duplicate boundary calls (`--chunk 50,000`).

We built `VarDictRust` (`v1.1.0`; empirical cloud campaign executed at `v1.0.6`) under the [rewrites.bio](https://rewrites.bio) prime directive to consolidate the pipeline into a memory-safe, single-binary Rust implementation. Its default mode (`--compat=vardictjava` / `--parity`) is byte- and float-equivalent (`ε = 1e-6`) to `VarDictJava` v1.8.3 on all valid inputs, eliminating runtime panics and bounding peak memory to `O(tile + halo)` regardless of chromosome length or sequencing depth. In `v1.1.0`, `VarDictRust` pairs this default parity baseline with `32` opt-in accuracy, SV/mate-pileup, somatic multi-allelic/amplicon, VCFv4.2/v4.3 schema, and modern-aligner (`--eqx`) corrections behind `--compat=off`, alongside clinical `--preset` modes (`wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, `ctdna`), `--amplicon-single-primer` (`-sp`), and all `18/18` consolidated `var2vcf` post-filters including `--vcf-all-alleles` (native consolidation of `var2vcf_*.pl -A` [1]; `Table 9`, `Figure 9`, `Supplementary Table S7`).

## Results

### Tier-1/2 Oracle Equivalence (Exact at `ε = 1e-6`, `HALF_EVEN`, Record-Aligned) Across Operational Modes and the 87-Configuration Sweep

Across our `60`-experiment campaign (`Table 1`, `Figure 2a`, `Supplementary Table S1`), we tested `VarDictRust` (`v1.0.6`) against `VarDictJava v1.8.3 (0886261)`. We checked both Tier-1 intermediate `.var` parity and Tier-2 VCF 4.2 parity. Tier-1 covers `Simple` `36/38`, `Amplicon` `38/40`, and `Somatic` `55/61` columns without/with `--fisher`. Tier-2 covers `chrom`, `pos`, `ref`, `alt`, `QUAL`, `FILTER`, `INFO` tags `SBF`, `ODDRATIO`, `SSF`, `MSI`, `MSILEN`, `SHIFT3`, `TYPE`, `STATUS`, and `FORMAT` genotypes. All comparisons use `ε = 1e-6` with `HALF_EVEN` rounding, record-aligned on CHROM/POS/REF/ALT.

Across the baseline ceiling, multi-region boundary deduplication, and tile-size invariance benchmarks, `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` with zero discrepancies (`Table 1`; `Supplementary Table S1`):

- **EGFR/MRE11A Amplicon (`-a 10:0.95`):** `VarDictRust` emitted all `2` variants in `0.01` s (`11.5` MB RSS) vs `3.00` s (`120.1` MB) for `VarDictJava` (`300`× faster, `10.4`× lower RSS). `vardictcpp` (`5.27` s, `119.7` MB) emits raw `.var` text instead of VCF in amplicon mode (`Table 8`; `Supplementary Table S6`).

- **NIST GIAB HG008 Somatic Slice:** `VarDictRust` matched all `18,426` candidate variants in `17.44` s (`502.8` MB RSS). `VarDictJava` took `79.94` s (`9.87` GiB RSS). That is `4.58`× faster at `20.1`× lower RSS (`vardictcpp`: `17.57` s, `554.5` MB).

- **HG002 Chromosome 20 Multi-Region Boundary:** `VarDictRust` matched all `2,572` variants in `0.28` s (`176.7` MB RSS). `VarDictJava` took `7.28` s (`1.50` GiB RSS). That is `26.0`× faster at `8.7`× lower RSS.

- **Tile-Size Invariance:** We swept four tile sizes (`500,000, 1,000,000, 2,000,000, 10,000,000` bp, i.e., `0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 10.0` Mbp; Methods) on SEQC2 HCC1395. Every size produced the exact same `5,090` variants as single-region `VarDictJava` (`4/4` `MATCH`).

The `13`-mode functional sweep (`Table 1`, `Supplementary Table S6`) covers seven call families. These are Simple (`--fisher` and R pipe), Somatic (`--fisher` and R pipe), Amplicon (`-a`), and Splice (`-i`). They include Structural Variant (`SV` and `--nosv`), CRAM (`-G`), and all-positions (`-f 0`) calling. `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` across `13/13` modes (`100%` Tier-1/2 parity, exact Clopper-Pearson `95%` CI `75.29%–100.00%`). On the `51.8` GiB HG002 GM24385 RNA-seq alignment (`Table 1`, `Supplementary Table S1`), `VarDictRust` `-i` splice mode matched all `12,668` `.var` and `.splice.tsv` exon-junction rows. It took `4.94` s (`n = 3` technical replicates, range `4.90–5.01` s) at `359.9` MB RSS. `VarDictJava` took `23.31` s (`23.03–23.70` s) at `4.79` GiB. That is `4.72`× faster at `13.6`× lower RSS. `vardictcpp` does not implement `-i` (`Supplementary Table S6`). `VarDictRust` also passed `17/17` extended coverage modalities (`95%` CI `80.49%–100.00%`). It passed `18/18` multimodal slices (`1,129` variants, `95%` CI `81.47%–100.00%`). Lossless BAM-vs-CRAM twin calling showed zero diff (`32.04` s, `297.7` MB RSS).

Our `87`-configuration parameter sweep (`Table 1`, `Supplementary Table S2`) spans four calling regimes across `12` suites: standard `-f 0.01`, WGS MRD `-f 0.001`, ultra-deep `45000`× MRD `-f 0.0005`, and unfiltered `-f 0` calling. `VarDictRust` achieved `87/87` (`100.00%`, `95%` CI `95.85%–100.00%`) exact Tier-1 `.var` and Tier-2 `.vcf` parity with `VarDictJava`. Specifically, all `84/87` standard BED sub-arms ($\le 1,000,000$ bp) matched untiled by default. The remaining `3/87` $> 1,000,000$ bp single-interval arms matched `100%` under single-window `--tile-size` (`--tile-size 10000000` on `10` Mbp `chr1` `-f 0`, `7,077` `.var` rows / `7,032` VCF variants; `--tile-size 250000000` on `249.25` Mbp `-R chr1:1-249250621`, `4,705` `.var` rows). Under default `100` kbp auto-tiling (`318.6` MB RSS on `-R chr1`), outputs differ only at cross-tile $> 100$ kbp SV/mate spans (`4` calls on `10` Mbp `chr1` `-f 0`, while suppressing `VarDictJava`'s cross-centromere `36`–`81` Mbp SV / `VD > DP` state leak on `249.25` Mbp `-R chr1`).

### Replication and Extension of the Wolff Acute Myeloid Leukemia Benchmark

We replicated the Wolff [11] acute myeloid leukemia (AML) targeted panel cohort (`SRR15006375`, `SRR15006376`, `SRR15006386`, `SRR15006540`, `SRR8657348`, and paired somatic `SRR15006376|SRR15006386`, spanning `11,732` to `773,667` BED intervals; `Table 2`, `Figure 2`, `Supplementary Table S3`) at `1` and `8` threads.

Across all `12` sample-by-thread configurations (`n = 3` technical replicates per engine), `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` calls. Coverage was `100%` of `.var` (`4,056`–`54,448` rows) and `.vcf` (`3,966`–`53,224` variants) calls (Tier-1/2 parity). `VarDictRust` achieved a `2.36`×–`5.56`× speedup and `4.3`×–`26.5`× lower peak RSS (`187.5`–`493.2` MB vs `2.03`–`7.44` GB for `VarDictJava`). Two-sided Mann-Whitney $U$ tests compared runtime ($U = 4.0$, $p = 9.735e-05$) and RSS ($U = 0.0$, $p = 3.658e-05$). A two-sided exact paired sign-flip permutation gave $p = 0.00049$ ($2^{-11}$; one-sided $p = 0.00024$ across $N = 12$ pairing `6` AML cohorts across `1` and `8` threads). On the largest `773,667`-interval panel (`SRR8657348`), `1`-thread `VarDictRust` (`154.98` s, range `154.77–155.12` s) beat both `VarDictJava` (`861.18` s, `5.56`×) and `vardictcpp` (`174.48` s). At `8` threads (`-th 8`), `vardictcpp` finished the pre-chunked AML panels in `12.85`–`31.66` s vs `39.63`–`135.43` s for `VarDictRust`. That gap reflects `vardictcpp` hardcoding `--nosv` and skipping `##contig` and `INFO/END` VCF schema validation (`Table 8`; `Supplementary Table S6`).

Replicating Table 3 of Wolff [11] on contiguous human chromosome 1 (`-R chr1:1-249250621`, `249.25` Mbp on `SRR8657348`; `Table 2`, `Figure 4b`, `Supplementary Table S1`) separates tile-bounded streaming (`VarDictRust`) from whole-interval allocation (`VarDictJava` and `vardictcpp`):

- **`VarDictJava`:** Crashed with `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError` at `31.08` s under a `-Xmx4g` heap (`4.16` GiB RSS). Under `-Xmx200g` it required `501.83` s (`n = 3` range `492.93–509.65` s) and `106.47` GiB peak RSS.

- **`vardictcpp`:** Required `105.89` s (`n = 3` range `105.07–106.58` s) and `10.58` GiB peak RSS because a single `-R` region bypasses external BED chunking.

- **`VarDictRust`:** Auto-tiled `-R chr1:1-249250621` into `100` kbp windows with `0.5` kbp halos. It finished in `9.75` s (`n = 3` range `9.64–9.81` s) at `318.6` MB RSS. That is `51.5`× faster and `342.2`× leaner than `VarDictJava` (`-Xmx200g`). It is `10.9`× faster and `34.0`× leaner than `vardictcpp`.

Against external callers (`FreeBayes`, `LoFreq`, `Mutect2`, `Octopus`, `Strelka2`; `Table 2`, `Supplementary Table S1`), `8`-thread `VarDictRust` on `SRR8657348` took `39.63` s (`493.2` MB). That beat `FreeBayes` (`57.14` s), `LoFreq` (`160.64` s), `Mutect2` (`1297.75` s), and `Octopus` (`1823.58` s). ### Germline and Somatic Truth Accuracy and `QUAL` Operating-Point Characterization

We evaluated `rtg vcfeval` [12] genotype-level precision, recall, and $F_1$ across germline and somatic truth sets (`Table 3`, `Figure 3`, `Supplementary Table S4`). On the hg19 HG002 chromosome 20 exome benchmark (`Table 3`), `VarDictRust` and `VarDictJava` produced identical SNV (`0.9981` precision, `0.9738` recall, $F_1 = 0.9858$) accuracy. The benchmark spans `2.31` Mbp with `8,233` candidate calls and `7,422` filtered calls. Indel accuracy was identical too (`0.9747` precision, `0.8283` recall, $F_1 = 0.8956$). They exceeded `Mutect2` (`SNV` $F_1 = 0.9651$, `Indel` $F_1 = 0.8241$). They matched `Octopus` (`SNV` $F_1 = 0.9925$), `FreeBayes` (`0.9867`), and `LoFreq` (`0.9803`).

`VarDictJava` defaults to a low-VAF threshold (`-f 0.01`). Unfiltered `PASS` callsets on `35`×–`60`× WGS/exome data therefore retain low-VAF candidates. Stratifying by `QUAL` separates them (`Table 3`, `Supplementary Table S4`, `Figure 3b`). Across all five GIAB and SEQC2 truth cohorts, `VarDictRust` and `VarDictJava` yielded identical `rtg vcfeval` ROC curves:

- **HG002 GRCh38 Slice (`NIST GIAB v4.2.1`):** Optimal `QUAL >= 128.000` filtering removes `97.5%` of unfiltered false positives (`22,458` to `553` FP). It keeps `0.9635` recall (`23,004` TP). - **SEQC2 HCC1395/HCC1395BL Somatic Exome (`v1.2.1`):** Across `623,444` candidate calls (`120,307` `PASS`), `QUAL >= 126.000` raises combined somatic $F_1$ from `0.1555` to `0.6905` (`794` TP, `293` FP, precision `0.7279`, recall `0.6567`). - **NIST GIAB HG008 Somatic Slice (`v0.3`):** Across `18,426` candidates (`11,358` `PASS`), `QUAL >= 176.000` raises combined $F_1$ from `0.3856` to `0.7696` (`593` TP, `38` FP). ### Bounded Memory Scaling, Multicore Work-Stealing, and Multi-Tenant Node Packing

We tested `VarDictRust`'s `O(threads × (tile + halo))` memory bound across four scaling benchmarks (`Table 4`, `Figure 4`, `Supplementary Table S3`):

1. **Four-Assay Performance Gate (`Table 4`, `Supplementary Table S3`):** Across `n = 3` technical replicates, `VarDictRust` held wall-time ratios to `0.0033`–`0.2214`. RSS ratios held to `0.0298`–`0.0978` relative to `VarDictJava`. Targets were EGFR/MRE11A amplicon (`0.01` s vs `3.07` s Java) and HG002 chr20 germline (`21.29` s vs `153.25` s). They included HG008 somatic (`17.11` s vs `77.28` s) and SEQC2 somatic exome (`277.52` s vs `4045.00` s).

2. **Whole-Exome Multicore Scaling (`1` to `32` Threads, `Table 4`, `Figure 4c`):** We used the SEQC2 HCC1395 whole exome (`339,698` total variants, `120,307` `PASS`, `n = 3` technical replicates). `VarDictRust` scaled from `812.75` s (`343.5` MB RSS) at `1` thread. At `8` threads it reached `118.90` s (`6.84`× self-speedup). At `32` threads it reached `67.86` s (`11.98`× self-speedup, `747.2` MB RSS). `VarDictJava` plateaued above `8` threads (`1814.63` s at `8` threads; `1991.64` s at `32` threads). Its heap grew from `1.79` GiB to `21.77` GiB. At `32` threads `VarDictRust` is `29.4`× faster and `29.8`× leaner than `VarDictJava`. It is `2.8`× leaner than `vardictcpp` at `2.06` GiB.

3. **Chromosome 1 Span Invariance (`Table 4`, `Figure 4d`):** Contiguous chromosome 1 span grew `250`× across `1,000,000`, `10,000,000`, `100,000,000`, and `248,956,422` bp (`1` to `249` Mbp). `VarDictRust`'s `249` Mbp-to-`10` Mbp RSS ratio stayed below `1.50`× at every thread count. Ratios were `1.18`× (`1.38` GiB) at `8` threads, `1.29`× (`2.31` GiB) at `16` threads, and `1.41`× (`3.69` GiB) at `32` threads. On `10` Mbp all-positions `-f 0` pileup (`7,032` variants), `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` in `19.58` s (`5.90` GiB RSS).

4. **Concurrent Node Packing (`Table 4`, `Figure 4e`):** Two concurrent `4`-core SEQC2 exome jobs finished in `211.38` s (`437.5` MB) and `211.05` s (`442.0` MB). That matches an isolated `4`-core run (`212.30` s). Combined RSS was `0.859` GiB. ### Full-Genome WGS Scaling: Unchunked Full Chromosomes Versus Pre-Chunked Callable Intervals

We compared all three engines at `32` threads across two GRCh38 whole genomes (`Table 5`, `Figure 5`): `130.8` GB NIST HG002 `2×250` bp germline WGS and `329.0` GB NIST HG008 `364`× (`195`× tumor / `169`× normal) paired somatic WGS (`VarDictRust` stayed well within `128` GiB `n2-standard-32` memory bounds, while `-Xmx200g` comparator baselines ran on `256` GiB `n2-highmem-32` nodes with identical Intel Xeon Platinum 8373C Ice Lake silicon). Each genome was run under two region protocols:

- **Unchunked Full Chromosomes (`chr1`–`chr22`, `22` autosomes):** On HG002, `VarDictJava` (`-Xmx200g`) aborted with `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError` at `218.83` GB RAM. `vardictcpp` finished in `18:50.80` at `32.68` GB RSS (`6.5`× higher than `VarDictRust`). It emitted `9,953,338` variants (`+309,569` duplicate chunk-boundary calls relative to `VarDictRust`'s `9,643,769`). `VarDictRust` finished all `22` autosomes in `25:16.61` at `5.05` GB RSS. On `364`× HG008 paired somatic WGS, `VarDictJava` crashed out of memory at `219.25` GB. `vardictcpp` reached `94.06` GB RSS (`7.6`× higher than `VarDictRust`) before timing out at `3` hours. `VarDictRust` finished in `2:49:52` at `12.43` GB RSS (`13,067,287` variants). These unchunked runs are completion results: neither Java nor C++ finished them.

- **Pre-Chunked Callable Intervals (`≤50` kbp GIAB BED):** On pre-split HG002 intervals (`2.51` Gbp), `VarDictRust` finished in `20:21.21` at `1.47` GB RSS. That is `9.25`× faster and `23.8`× leaner than `VarDictJava` (`3:08:15`, `34.96` GB). It is `19.8`× leaner than `vardictcpp` at `29.03` GB in `11:40.98`. `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` across all `8,111,171` variants (`vardictcpp` differed by `394` at `8,111,565`). It reached `rtg vcfeval` $F_1 = 0.9651$ (`0.9738` precision, `0.9566` recall; unfiltered $F_1 = 0.6624$). On pre-split HG008 intervals (`2.37` Gbp), `VarDictJava` (`64.01` GB) timed out at `4` hours (`4:00:00`). `vardictcpp` (`21.66` GB) timed out at `3` hours (`3:00:00`). `VarDictRust` finished in `1:45:55` at `4.99` GB RSS (`10,195,118` variants; somatic $F_1 = 0.6547$ on `GIAB v0.3` and `0.6585` on `GIAB v0.4`). This HG008 run is completion-only: no oracle finished it.

### Cross-Platform Sequencing Chemistry Generalization and 70-Cell GA4GH v3.5 Stratification

We evaluated `10` short-read sequencing chemistries across the full `64.44` Mbp human chromosome 20 (`Table 6`, `Figure 6`, `Supplementary Table S4`, `Supplementary Table S5`). The panel holds `4` HG002 germline chemistries (`Illumina`, `Element AVITI`, `MGI DNBSEQ`, `Ultima Genomics UG100`). It also holds `6` HG008 paired somatic chemistries (`Illumina`, `Element AVITI`, `PacBio Onso`, `Roche SBX`, `Ultima Genomics ppmSeq`, `Ultima Genomics UG100`).

`VarDictRust` completed all `20` standard (`-f 0.01`) and all-frequency (`-f 0`) chromosome 20 runs (`100%` completion). `VarDictJava` completed `11/20` runs and timed out on `9` deep runs (`Table 6`). SNV $F_1$ was identical across HG002 germline (`0.6622–0.9598` standard; `0.6622–0.9618` `UG-HCR`). ### Unlocking GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100, CMRG v1.00, and Mosaic v1.10 Discovery via Unchunked Calling

We compared full-genome HG002 callsets from unchunked chromosomes (`chr1`–`chr22`) versus pre-chunked `≤50` kbp `GIAB v4.2.1` intervals across three NIST truth benchmarks (`Table 7`, `Figure 7`):

1. **`NIST GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100` (`2.74` Gbp, diploid Telomere-to-Telomere Q100 assembly truth):** Because `VarDictJava` runs out of memory on unchunked chromosomes, legacy workflows restrict calling to pre-chunked `GIAB v4.2.1` intervals. Those cover `2.51` Gbp and miss `+0.23` Gbp of segmental duplications, pericentromeric satellites, and complex loci added in `v5.0q`. On `GIAB v4.2.1`, unchunked and pre-chunked calling matched (`SNV` recall `0.9577` vs `0.9576`, `SNV` $F_1 = 0.6639$, `Indel` $F_1 = 0.6928$). On `GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100`, streaming unchunked full chromosomes with `VarDictRust` raised genome-wide SNV recall by `+5.01%` (`0.8866` to `0.9367`, $F_1$ from `0.6461` to `0.6650`). Indel recall rose by `+17.26%` (`0.5281` to `0.7007`, $F_1$ from `0.5487` to `0.6551`).

2. **`NIST Challenging Medically Relevant Genes` (`CMRG v1.00`, `273` clinical genes):** Many of the `273` CMRG genes lie outside the `v4.2.1` `50` kbp mask. Unchunked `VarDictRust` calling increased CMRG SNV recall by `+17.47%` (`0.7173` to `0.8920`, $F_1$ from `0.6132` to `0.6791`). Indel recall rose by `+27.98%` (`0.4904` to `0.7702`, $F_1$ from `0.5316` to `0.6937`).

3. **`NIST Mosaic SNV v1.10` (`85` validated low-VAF mosaic SNVs in HG002, VAF `1%–30%`):** `VarDictRust` and `VarDictJava` detected `84/85` (`98.82%`, `95%` CI `93.62%–99.97%`) validated mosaic variants (`Table 7`).

### Deep-Learning and Commercial Caller Comparison, Low-VAF Titration, and Supply-Chain Hardening

We compared `VarDictRust` against contemporary deep-learning, graph, and haplotype somatic callers. Three benchmarks cover them (`Table 3`, `Table 7`, `Table 8`, `Figure 3d`, `Figure 7c`, `Figure 8`, `Supplementary Table S6`):

- **`11`-Caller Benchmark on `NIST GIAB HG008 v0.4` (`27` evaluations; `Table 7`, `Figure 7c`):** We compared `VarDictRust` (`v1.0.6`) against official NIST GIAB and local callsets. Callers were `DRAGEN v4.2.4` (`Graph` and `Linear`) [21], `DeepSomatic v1.6.0` [22], `ClairS v0.4.1` [23], `Lancet2` (`NYGC`) [24], `Hartwig SAGE` [25], `Ultima DeepVariant Somatic` [6], `Mutect2` [8], `Strelka2` [4], and `LoFreq` [7]. Scopes were full-genome (`All` and `NoGI`) and slice. On full-genome HG008 `v0.4` (`All`), `VarDictRust` reached `40.11%` somatic indel recall (`Indel` $F_1 = 0.4898$, `SNV` $F_1 = 0.7886$). On `NoGI` it reached `48.32%` indel recall (`Indel` $F_1 = 0.5616$, `SNV` $F_1 = 0.8028$). This exceeded `ClairS v0.4.1` (`13.02%` indel recall, $F_1 = 0.2298$) and `DeepSomatic v1.6.0` (`18.45%`, $F_1 = 0.3072$). It also exceeded `Lancet2` (`21.79%`, $F_1 = 0.3568$) and `Ultima DeepVariant Somatic` (`24.59%`, $F_1 = 0.3900$). It approached FPGA-accelerated `DRAGEN v4.2.4 Graph` (`43.23%`) on standard CPUs.

- **Controlled *in silico* Tumor-Purity Titration (`20%` to `1%` Purity / `10.0% to 0.5%` VAF; `Supplementary Table S4`, `Figure 3d`):** We built five HG008 tumor-in-normal admixtures. Purities were `20%`, `10%`, `5%`, `2%`, and `1%` with expected clonal VAF `10.0%`, `5.0%`, `2.5%`, `1.0%`, and `0.5%`. Each was called at `-f 0.002`. `VarDictRust` matched `VarDictJava` at every purity. It ran `7.6×–7.7×` faster (`8.53–8.76` s vs `65.16–66.76` s) at `14.7×–16.5×` lower RSS (`780,628–859,564` KB vs `12,316,396–13,765,960` KB). At `5%` purity (`2.5%` VAF), `VarDictRust` recalled `36.80%` of SNVs ($F_1 = 0.4215$). Indel recall was `10.54%` ($F_1 = 0.1422$) vs `18.91%` (`Strelka2`), `9.88%` (`Mutect2`), and `0.51%` (`LoFreq`). At `1%` purity (`0.5%` VAF), `VarDictRust` retained `4.26%` SNV recall ($F_1 = 0.0569$). That is `25.1`× higher recall and `16.7`× higher $F_1$ than `Mutect2` (`0.17%`, $F_1 = 0.0034$) and `Strelka2` (`0.17%`).

- **Supply-Chain Hardening and `vardictcpp` Audit (`Table 8`, `Figure 8`, `Supplementary Table S6`):** Compiling `VarDictRust` to a `4.56` MiB static Linux binary (`4.46` MiB dynamic `glibc`) removes `102` runtime Conda packages (`OpenJDK`, `R`, `Perl`). That resolves all 12 `vardictcpp` functional and statistical divergences (`Table 8`).

## Discussion

### Scientific Software Consolidation Under the `rewrites.bio` Specification
Rewriting a clinical variant caller for speed risks changing Smith-Waterman tie-breaking, Phred quality offsets, floating-point rounding, or strand-bias p-values, which forces clinical laboratories to re-validate pipelines. Conversely, line-by-line transliteration preserves memory growth and crash bugs. `VarDictRust` separates **default oracle parity (`--compat=vardictjava`) and crash/memory robustness** from **opt-in algorithmic corrections (`--compat=off`)** under the [rewrites.bio](https://rewrites.bio) specification. In default mode (`v1.0.6` through `v1.1.0`), `VarDictRust` reproduces the exact allele calls, `FILTER` flags, somatic `STATUS` classifications, and `HALF_EVEN`-rounded Fisher exact statistics of `VarDictJava v1.8.3 (0886261)` (`Table 1`, `Supplementary Table S2`), while eliminating JVM `OutOfMemoryError` failures, `CigarModifier` bounds panics (`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`), and `java.util.HashMap` row-order non-determinism (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#282`). In `v1.1.0`, opt-in `--compat=off` (`CompatProfile`, stamping `##VarDictRustCompat=off` without per-bug `--fix` CLI flag sprawl) activates `32` workspace-tested corrections across five functional families (`Table 9`, `Supplementary Table S7`). The schema and SV families enforce VCFv4.2/v4.3 `FORMAT/AD` `Number=R` cardinality, non-SV `INFO/END` omission, IUPAC/`SHIFT3` FASTA concordance, canonical `0/1` diploid genotypes, 1-bp symbolic `<DEL>`/`<DUP>`/`<INV>` anchoring, signed `SVLEN`, `-L` length bounds, and lazy mate-window pileups. The calling, alignment, and accounting families add multi-allelic somatic continuation (`joachimwolff/VarDictJava#4`), paired tumor-normal amplicon calling (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#378`), directional `--adaptor` soft-clip screening (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#240`), outer hard-clip stripping, homopolymer CIGAR coalescing, `--eqx` (`=`/`X`) read-length accounting, `-p` pileup coordinate retention, and `VD <= DP <= RD + VD` depth/strand/IEEE-754 `HALF_EVEN`/coordinate-sorted reconciliation—paired with `--preset` (`wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, `ctdna`), `--vcf-all-alleles`, and `--amplicon-single-primer` (`-sp`) [27]. Default mode (`--compat=vardictjava` / `--parity`) preserves upstream `VarDictJava` output quirks across `v1.x`, while specification-gated `NM` over-filtering (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#62`), realignment-drop (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#363`), `<1%` low-AF noise (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#381`), and indel/delins boundary-normalization (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#264`) heuristics remain deferred to `v1.1.1+`, and native UMI/duplex consensus (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#374`), `F1R2`/`F2R1` orientation-bias modeling, and `--preset hifi|ont` profiles to `v2.0` (`Table 8`, `Supplementary Note 3`).

### Impact of Bounded Streaming on T2T-Q100 and CMRG Discovery
Whole-interval heap allocation in `VarDictJava` and `vardictcpp` has historically restricted whole-genome `VarDict` calling to pre-chunked `≤50` kbp BED masks (`GIAB v4.2.1`) or external `50` kbp BED-splitting scripts (`Table 5`). Keeping intervals $\le 1,000,000$ bp untiled and partitioning spans $> 1,000,000$ bp into `100` kbp tiles with `0.5` kbp realignment halos (`Table 1`) allows `VarDictRust` to call unchunked full chromosomes in `5.05`–`12.43` GB RAM. Removing the `50` kbp BED mask recovers `+0.23` Gbp of segmental duplications and complex loci in `NIST GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100` (`+5.01%` SNV recall, `+17.26%` indel recall) and raises recall across the `273` `NIST CMRG v1.00` genes (`+17.47%` SNV, `+27.98%` indel; `Table 7`).

### Trade-Offs Against Deep-Learning, Commercial, and C++ Callers
On `364`× `HG008 v0.4` somatic WGS (`Table 7`, `Figure 7c`), deep-learning (`DeepSomatic`, `ClairS`, `Ultima DeepVariant Somatic`) and graph/haplotype (`DRAGEN`, `Lancet2`, `Hartwig SAGE`, `Mutect2`, `Strelka2`) callers achieve higher SNV precision in repetitive sequence. `VarDictRust`, however, achieves higher somatic indel recall (`40.11%`) than `ClairS`, `DeepSomatic`, `Lancet2`, and `Ultima DeepVariant Somatic`. It retains `25.1`× higher SNV recall than `Mutect2` and `Strelka2` at `0.5%` VAF (`1%` purity; `Supplementary Table S4`) on commodity CPUs without GPUs or FPGAs. On pre-chunked `773,667`-interval panels (`SRR8657348`, `8` threads), `vardictcpp` finishes raw `.var` calling in `24.68` s vs `39.63` s for `VarDictRust`. That gap reflects `vardictcpp` hardcoding `--nosv` and skipping `##contig` and `INFO/END` VCF validation (`Table 8`). `VarDictRust` instead completes every unchunked chromosome run and all `20` deep chromosome 20 runs with full SV and VCF output.

### Operational Boundary Conditions
Four settings govern production deployment:

1. **Multi-Megabase Single-Region Calling (`--tile-size` vs. `100` kbp Auto-Tiling):** Intervals $\le 1,000,000$ bp run untiled by default. On unchunked $> 1,000,000$ bp regions, default `100` kbp auto-tiling bounds `-R chr1:1-249250621` memory to `318.6` MB (`Table 2`). It also prevents `VarDictJava`'s cross-centromere `36`–`81` Mbp SV pairing and `VD = 74 > DP` state leak (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#92`). Passing `--tile-size` equal to the region span reproduces `VarDictJava`'s single-window output byte-for-byte. Use `--tile-size 10000000` on `10` Mbp `chr1` `-f 0`. Use `--tile-size 250000000` on `249.25` Mbp `-R chr1` (`7,077` `.var` / `7,032` `.vcf` and `4,705` `.var` rows; `Table 1`, `Supplementary Table S2`).

2. **`QUAL` Filtering on Standard-Depth Germline WGS:** Because `-f 0.01` targets low-VAF somatic discovery, `35`×–`60`× germline WGS pipelines need filtering. Filter at `QUAL >= 128.000` ($F_1 = 0.9699$ vs `0.6622` unfiltered, removing `97.5%` of false positives; `Table 3`, `Supplementary Table S4`). Alternatively set `-f` to `0.10`–`0.20`.

3. **Dual-Mode `--compat=vardictjava|off` Policy:** Default invocations across `v1.x` (`--compat=vardictjava` / `--parity`) preserve upstream `VarDictJava` v1.8.3 output quirks (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#62`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#92`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#116`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#292`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#342`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#369`, `AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#398`) for zero-diff drop-in migration. In `v1.1.0`, opt-in `--compat=off` stamps `##VarDictRustCompat=off` in VCF headers and activates `32` `CompatProfile` fixes (`Supplementary Table S7`), while `--preset` (`wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, `ctdna`; `##VarDictRustPreset=<PRESET>`) configures short-read clinical workflows, `--vcf-all-alleles` (`--all-alleles`) enables multi-allelic VCF emission, and `--amplicon-single-primer` (`-sp`) supports single-primer extension panels (`Table 9`).

4. **Short- and Medium-Read Chemistry Scope vs. Long-Read Sequencing:** `Table 6` evaluates `10` short- and medium-read sequencing-by-synthesis, sequencing-by-binding (`PacBio Onso`, `~150` bp paired-end short reads; cf. external NIST `PacBio HiFi` `DeepSomatic` and `ClairS` callsets in `Table 7`), and flow-based chemistries rather than `PacBio HiFi` or `Oxford Nanopore` (`ONT`) long reads. Upstream `VarDictJava` v1.8.3 omits `=` and `X` sequence-match/mismatch CIGAR operators (emitted by `minimap2 --eqx` and `pbmm2`) from `CigarParser` read-length accumulation (`rlen1 = 0`, yielding negative `PMEAN` and dropping calls on `=`/`X` alignments), enforces short-read per-read mismatch (`-m 8`), `var2vcf` mean-mismatch (`NM`), and indel-size (`-I 50` bp) defaults that even `15`–`20` kbp `M`-operator alignments exceed, and does not chain split-read `SA:Z` supplementary alignments across multi-kilobase reads. Benchmarking unmodified strict-parity presets on `15`–`20` kbp `HiFi` or `ONT` alignments would therefore conflate upstream short-read algorithmic parameterization with engine implementation quality. In `VarDictRust` `v1.1.0`, native `=`/`X` (`--eqx`) CIGAR read-length and coalescing support (`eqx_cigar_read_length`) ships alongside all `32` `CompatProfile` corrections under opt-in `--compat=off` (`Table 9`, `Supplementary Table S7`), while default `--compat=vardictjava` (`--parity`, certified fingerprint `b18477ae0a7b07c1`) preserves strict `VarDictJava v1.8.3` parity; short-read clinical presets (`--preset` across `wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, and `ctdna`) ship in `v1.1.0`, whereas dedicated `--preset hifi|ont` long-read profiles, native UMI/duplex consensus (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#374`), and `F1R2`/`F2R1` orientation-bias modeling remain slated for `v2.0`.

## Conclusions

`VarDictRust` (`v1.1.0`; `60`-experiment cloud benchmark archived at `v1.0.6`) consolidates the `VarDictJava -> R -> Perl` pipeline into a single static Rust binary. It holds exact Tier-1/2 oracle parity (`ε = 1e-6`, `HALF_EVEN`, record-aligned) across `13` operational modes, `10` sequencing chemistries (parity on all completing paired runs), and `70` completed Rust GA4GH `v3.5` truth evaluations. Bounding memory to `O(tile + halo)` delivers `51.5`× faster chromosome-scale calling (`342.2`× lower RSS) and `29.4`× faster `32`-thread whole-exome calling (`29.8`× lower RSS), unlocking unchunked whole-genome discovery across `GIAB v5.0q T2T-Q100` and `CMRG v1.00`, while opt-in `v1.1.0` `--compat=off` and `--preset` modes resolve targeted VCFv4.2/v4.3 schema/canonicalization defects (`19/19` `bcftools norm` pass) and rescue subclonal diploid somatic calls (`Table 9`, `Figure 9`).

## Methods

### Consolidated Rust Architecture and Coordinate Type Safety
`VarDictRust` (certified release `v1.1.0`, providing default `--compat=vardictjava` parity alongside opt-in `--compat=off`, `--preset` across `wgs`, `wes`, `amplicon`, and `ctdna`, `-sp`, and `--vcf-all-alleles`) is a two-crate Rust workspace: `vardictrust-core` (library engine, compiled with `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and zero `unwrap()`/`expect()`/`panic!()` calls outside unit tests) and `vardictrust` (`clap` CLI exposing `92` flags across `7` functional groups, including all `18` consolidated `var2vcf` post-filters, `--vcf-all-alleles`, `--preset`, `--compat=vardictjava|off`, multithreaded BGZF `--output` streaming, and native `--tbi`/`--csi` indexing). Distinct `Pos0` (`0`-based half-open BAM/CRAM coordinates from `noodles`) and `Pos1` (`1`-based inclusive VCF/VarDict coordinates) newtypes enforce explicit `.to_one_based()` and `.to_zero_based()` conversions. Sequences and qualities are processed as byte slices (`&[u8]`) with Phred+33 saturation arithmetic (`Phred(byte.saturating_sub(33))`). Continuous verification spans `859` automated Cargo workspace tests (`625` `vardictrust-core` unit tests), `37` harness diagnostic self-tests, and in-process `cargo-fuzz` hostile-input harnesses.

### Bounded Tile-Plus-Halo Streaming and Deterministic Realignment
Intervals $\le 1,000,000$ bp execute as a single untiled window. Intervals $> 1,000,000$ bp (or with explicit `--tile-size`) are partitioned into non-overlapping ownership tiles $[T_k^{\text{start} }, T_k^{\text{end} })$ of default width `100` kbp. Each tile is padded on both sides by a `0.5` kbp realignment halo (`0.5` kbp in `-i` splice mode; `Figure 2b`, `Figure 4d`). A `Rayon` work-stealing pool streams alignments overlapping $[T_k^{\text{start} } - H, T_k^{\text{end} } + H)$ via `noodles` (multi-threaded BGZF BAM and reference-decoded CRAM `-G`). It runs CIGAR modification (`cigar/modify.rs`), pileup accumulation (`cigar/parse.rs`), local realignment (`realign.rs`), SV assembly (`sv.rs`), and variant finalization (`tovars.rs`). It emits only variants whose canonical `Pos1` start falls in $[T_k^{\text{start} }, T_k^{\text{end} })$. Per-tile pileup maps drop immediately upon tile completion (RAII), whole-stream VCF candidate accumulation spills to disk above `8` MiB (`SpillableVcfAccumulator`), and indexed BAM/CRAM decoding pairs pre-validated `.bai`/`.csi` structural and size bounds (`512` MiB `.bai`; `256` MiB compressed/decompressed `.csi` caps) with singleflight CRAM container caching (`16` MiB per container across a `160` MiB `4`-shard LRU cache), a `512` MiB shared FASTA reference cache, two-slot paired somatic `CRAM_META_TLS` state, and `SeekWindowReader` slice validation. Where `VarDictJava` output order depends on `java.util.HashMap` bucket iteration (`AstraZeneca-NGS/VarDictJava#282`), `VarDictRust` ports OpenJDK 8 `HashMap` spread, bin, resize, and `TreeNode` Red-Black treeification semantics in `JavaHashMap`, and uses `IndexMap` (`LinkedHashMap`) and `BTreeMap` (`TreeMap`) elsewhere.

### In-Process Statistical Post-Processing and Banker's Rounding (`HALF_EVEN`)
`VarDictRust` computes hypergeometric two-sided Fisher exact p-values and conditional maximum-likelihood odds ratios (`uniroot` zero-in with tolerance $\text{ulp}(1)^{0.25}$) natively in Rust (`stats/fisher.rs`), followed by VCF 4.2 (`VCFv4.2`) formatting (`output/`), replacing R (`teststrandbias.R` / `testsomatic.R`) and Perl (`var2vcf_valid.pl` / `var2vcf_paired.pl`). Default mode uses a cleanroom saddlepoint hypergeometric kernel (`stats/hypergeom.rs`, Catherine Loader 2000 binomial deviance and Stirling error with extended-precision `f80_sum` accumulation), whereas `--fisher` replicates `VarDictJava`'s Apache Commons Math 3 `SaddlePointExpansion` / `HypergeometricDistribution` path; hypergeometric supports exceeding `128` MiB ($2^{24}$ points) switch from dense `logdc` tables to unimodal `ln P(j) - ln P(mode) >= -750` active-window summation. Floating-point output fields apply IEEE 754 round-half-to-even (`round_half_even`) at exact upstream field precisions—`5` decimal places for `SBF`, `ODDRATIO`, and `SSF`; `4` decimal places for `AF`, `AdjAF`, and `HIAF`; `3` decimal places for `MSI` and `SN`; and `1` decimal place for `QMEAN`, `PMEAN`, `MQ`, and `NM` feeding integer `QUAL`—matching R `round()` and Java `DecimalFormat` (`HALF_EVEN`) in both no-`--fisher` (trailing zeros kept) and `--fisher` (trailing zeros stripped) modes within `ε = 1e-6` ($10^{-6}$).

### Cloud Benchmarking, Reproducibility Protocol, and Statistical Analysis
All `60` cloud benchmark cells ran on Google Cloud Platform (`GCP n2-standard-32 (Intel Xeon Platinum 8373C Ice Lake, 32 vCPUs, 128 GiB RAM, 4x375 GB NVMe RAID0)`, `32` vCPUs, `128` GiB RAM, with `-Xmx200g` full-WGS comparator baselines provisioned on `n2-highmem-32` `256` GiB RAM nodes sharing identical Intel Xeon Platinum 8373C Ice Lake silicon; `1.5` TB local NVMe RAID0 scratch; pinned to `VarDictRust` `v1.0.6`, fingerprint `5865a2a0b24a8c17`, exact commands and SHA-256 manifests in `Supplementary Methods` and `Supplementary Table S1`); subsequent releases through `v1.1.0` (`fingerprint` `b18477ae0a7b07c1`) preserve zero-diff default oracle equivalence across all certified cohorts. Timed cells ran `n = 3` independent technical replicates under `/usr/bin/time -v` to record wall-clock seconds and `Maximum resident set size (kbytes)`. Wall-clock timeouts surface as exit code `rc=124` (`3`-hour limit); JVM heap exhaustion surfaces as `rc=3` (`OutOfMemoryError`). Binary concordance proportions use exact two-sided Clopper-Pearson `95%` binomial CIs ($\alpha = 0.05$); see `Supplementary Note 3` for the Beta-inverse derivation and `--compat=vardictjava|off` release governance. Runtime and RSS across the `12` AML panel configurations were compared using two-sided Mann-Whitney $U$ tests and two-sided exact paired sign-flip permutation tests ($2^{-11} = 0.00049$; one-sided $2^{-12} = 0.00024$). Cloud truth concordance (`Tables 3–7`) was evaluated with `rtg vcfeval` (`v3.12.1`, allele-presence `--squash-ploidy` on somatic cohorts) against official NIST GIAB and SEQC2 high-confidence BED masks (`HG002 v4.2.1`, `HG002 v5.0q T2T-Q100`, `CMRG v1.00`, `Mosaic v1.10`, `HG005 v4.2.1`, `SEQC2 v1.2.1`, and `HG008 v0.3`/`v0.4`), whereas `Table 9` Domain 2 evaluated ploidy-strict diploid `rtg vcfeval` without `--squash-ploidy`. *In silico* tumor-purity titrations (`20%`, `10%`, `5%`, `2%`, `1%`) were built via seed-controlled `samtools view -s` subsampling and merging of HG008 tumor and normal BAMs (`Supplementary Methods`).

The `v1.1.0` opt-in `--compat=off` and `--preset` evaluation (`Figure 9`, `Table 9`, `Supplementary Table S7`; `benchmarks/v110-compat-study/`, pinned to `VarDictRust` `v1.1.0`, source fingerprint `b18477ae0a7b07c1`, performance fingerprint `5c41be8047133842`) executes `6` host-independent deterministic callset, targeted `CompatProfile` VCF schema/canonicalization/accounting, FASTA reference-concordance (`bcftools norm --check-ref e`, `bcftools` `v1.24`, `samtools` `v1.24`, `tabix` `v1.24`) [26], `QIAseq V3` single-primer extension (`ENA SRP295113`) [27], and diploid `rtg vcfeval` (`rtg-tools` `v3.13`, invoked with `--all-records --vcf-score-field=QUAL` and without `--squash-ploidy`) [12] verification domains via `paper/collect_v110_compat_metrics.py`, recording zero host-dependent wall-clock or RSS measurements so single-host cloud timing isolation is strictly preserved (`Supplementary Methods 1.8`). Clopper-Pearson `95%` CIs in `Table 9` and `Results` are computed strictly over independent biological truth-set variant denominators (`SEQC2` somatic truth variants, `GIAB HG008 v0.4` somatic truth variants, and `QIAseq` `KnownPositives`), never over correlated per-callset VCF schema rows.

A single-command paired somatic `VarDictRust` invocation replacing `VarDictJava | testsomatic.R | var2vcf_paired.pl` (or `teststrandbias.R | var2vcf_valid.pl` in single-sample mode) is:
```bash
# Single-binary VarDictRust GERMLINE/SOMATIC calling with native Fisher exact test & BGZF+Tabix VCF emission:
vardictrust -G GRCh38.fa -f 0.01 -N "TUMOR|NORMAL" \
  -b "tumor.bam|normal.bam" -th 32 --fisher -z -c 1 -S 2 -E 3 -g 4 \
  --vcf-pval 0.05 --output calls.vcf.gz --tbi target.bed
```
